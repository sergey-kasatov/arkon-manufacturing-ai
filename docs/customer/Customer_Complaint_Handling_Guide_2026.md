# Arkon Manufacturing: Customer Complaint Handling Guide 2026

Document: Customer Complaint Handling Guide. Version 2026.1. Effective 2026-09-08.
Status: approved for customer use. Owner: Arkon Customer Quality.
This version supersedes the Customer Complaint Handling Guide 2025 (version 2025.2),
which is withdrawn and must no longer be used.

Arkon Manufacturing is a fictional company and this guide describes a simulated
quality process. No real supplier, customer, part or person is described here.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This guide explains how Arkon handles a quality notice or a customer complaint
about parts Arkon has delivered. It tells a customer's quality engineer what
happens at each stage, what Arkon commits to at each stage, what the customer
receives, and what Arkon asks the customer to do.

A quality notice is raised by Arkon. Arkon raises a quality notice when its own
production monitoring flags a risk that delivered or in-production parts may be
affected by a defect or a process deviation. A complaint is raised by the
customer. A complaint is a customer's report that delivered parts do not meet the
agreed specification.

Arkon handles a quality notice and a complaint under the same process, with the
same stages, the same commitments and the same reference format. This guide uses
the word notice for both unless a rule applies to only one of them.

## 2. The reference

Every quality notice and every complaint receives one Arkon reference. The Arkon
reference has the format ARK-INC-#####, for example ARK-INC-00348. The reference
is assigned when the notice is logged at Arkon, and a notice is not on record
until it has a reference.

The reference is the key to everything that follows. Quote the reference in every
message about the notice. The Customer Quality Desk answers status questions by
reference only. One reference covers one notice; a second finding on the same
parts receives its own reference.

A reference that Arkon does not recognise is answered as not on record. A
reference that is not on record has either not been logged yet or has been
quoted incorrectly; check the reference against the acknowledgement message.

## 3. The five stages of a notice

Every notice moves through five stages. The stage is shown to the customer as a
stage number out of five, a stage name and a customer status. The table lists
the stages, the customer status shown at each stage, the next step Arkon owes at
each stage and the time Arkon commits to for that next step.

| Stage | Stage name | Customer status | Next step Arkon owes | Arkon's commitment for the next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 of 5 | Received and logged | Received | Acknowledgement by the Arkon quality team | Critical: 15 minutes. High: 1 hour. All other notices: 24 hours (one business day) |
| 2 of 5 | Investigation opened | Under investigation | Containment decision | 24 hours from the acknowledgement |
| 3 of 5 | Containment measures active | Containment in place | Root cause and corrective action | 240 hours (10 days) from the containment decision |
| 4 of 5 | Corrective action implemented | Corrective action implemented | Effectiveness check and closure | 120 hours (5 days) from the corrective action |
| 5 of 5 | Closed | Closed, or Closed, no defect confirmed | None | None |

### Stage 1 of 5: Received and logged

At stage 1 the notice has been logged at Arkon and has its reference. The
customer status at stage 1 is Received. The next step Arkon owes at stage 1 is
the acknowledgement by the Arkon quality team. The acknowledgement confirms that
a named Arkon role has taken the notice and started work on it.

Arkon's commitment for the acknowledgement depends on the severity Arkon assigns
to the notice when it is logged. A notice Arkon classifies as critical is
acknowledged within 15 minutes. A notice Arkon classifies as high is acknowledged
within 1 hour. Every other notice is acknowledged within 24 hours, which is one
business day. The status answer for a notice shows the acknowledgement date that
applies to that notice.

### Stage 2 of 5: Investigation opened

At stage 2 the Arkon quality team has acknowledged the notice and is
investigating it. The customer status at stage 2 is Under investigation. The next
step Arkon owes at stage 2 is the containment decision. The containment decision
states whether suspect parts are held back, sorted, reworked or released, at
Arkon and at the customer.

Arkon's commitment for the containment decision is 24 hours from the
acknowledgement.

### Stage 3 of 5: Containment measures active

At stage 3 the containment decision has been taken and the containment measures
are in place. The customer status at stage 3 is Containment in place. The next
step Arkon owes at stage 3 is the root cause and corrective action. The root
cause and corrective action is reported to the customer in an 8D-style report.

Arkon's commitment for the root cause and corrective action is 240 hours, which
is 10 days, from the containment decision. If a containment measure does not
hold, the notice returns to stage 3, and the 240-hour commitment counts again
from the moment the notice returned to stage 3.

### Stage 4 of 5: Corrective action implemented

At stage 4 the corrective action has been implemented at Arkon. The customer
status at stage 4 is Corrective action implemented. The next step Arkon owes at
stage 4 is the effectiveness check and closure. The effectiveness check confirms
on delivered parts that the corrective action works.

Arkon's commitment for the effectiveness check and closure is 120 hours, which
is 5 days, from the corrective action.

### Stage 5 of 5: Closed

At stage 5 the notice is closed. The customer status at stage 5 is Closed when
a defect was confirmed and corrected. The customer status at stage 5 is Closed,
no defect confirmed when the investigation found that the delivered parts meet
the specification. There is no next step at stage 5. A closed notice is final;
a new finding on the same parts receives a new reference.

## 4. What the customer receives, and when

Arkon sends the customer a written message at each stage change. All messages
are written; Arkon does not rely on telephone calls for any stage change.

- Acknowledgement message, when the notice moves from stage 1 to stage 2. It
  confirms the reference, the parts concerned and the Arkon role handling the
  notice.
- Containment notice, when the notice moves to stage 3. It states the
  containment decision and what the customer is asked to do with suspect parts.
- Root cause and corrective action report, in the 8D format, when the notice
  moves to stage 4. It states the root cause, the corrective action and the
  date from which corrected parts are delivered.
- Closure letter, when the notice moves to stage 5. It states the outcome, Closed
  or Closed, no defect confirmed, and the effectiveness check result.

Messages go to the customer contact named on the notice, by e-mail or through
the Arkon customer portal, in the language the customer contact has asked for,
German or English.

## 5. Commitment dates and overdue notices

Arkon's commitment for the next step is counted from the moment the notice
entered its current stage. The status answer for a notice shows the next step,
the date and time by which Arkon has committed to it, and whether that date has
passed.

A notice is overdue when the commitment date for its next step has passed and
the next step has not been recorded. An overdue notice is escalated inside Arkon
without the customer having to ask. The escalation ladder and the roles at each
level are described in the Quality Commitments and Escalation Contacts document.

## 6. Checking the status of a notice

The customer can ask the Arkon Customer Quality Desk for the status of a notice
at any time. The desk needs the reference to answer. For a reference on record
the desk tells the customer eight things and nothing else:

1. The reference.
2. The type of the notice.
3. The date and time the notice was received.
4. The customer status.
5. The stage, as a number out of five and a stage name.
6. The next step Arkon owes, the date and time Arkon has committed to for it,
   and whether that date has passed.
7. The date and time of the last update.
8. The date and time the notice was closed, if it is closed.

The desk does not tell the customer the names of the Arkon employees working on
a notice, the internal evidence behind a notice, the internal severity codes,
the details of Arkon's inspection or monitoring systems, or anything about
another customer's notice. These are Arkon internal information and are not
released through the desk.

## 7. What Arkon asks of the customer

- Report a complaint with the items listed in the Complaint Submission
  Checklist, so that the investigation can start without a query back.
- Quote the reference in every message about the notice.
- Quarantine suspect parts at the customer as soon as the notice is received,
  and do not rework, scrap or return suspect parts before the containment
  decision.
- Provide samples of suspect parts when Arkon asks for them for the
  investigation.
- Name one customer contact and one written channel for the notice.

## 8. Commercial settlement

Costs arising from a notice, such as sorting, rework, credit notes or returns,
are agreed in writing between the customer and the Arkon customer quality
contact after the closure of the notice. The Customer Quality Desk gives no
commercial commitment. The desk can calculate figures from numbers the customer
supplies, such as a reject rate in parts per million, but a calculation by the
desk is not a commercial commitment by Arkon.

## 9. Definitions

- Quality notice: a notice raised by Arkon about a risk to delivered or
  in-production parts.
- Complaint: a report raised by the customer that delivered parts do not meet
  the agreed specification.
- Reference: the Arkon reference of a notice, format ARK-INC-#####.
- Containment: the measures that stop suspect parts from reaching the customer's
  production line, at Arkon and at the customer.
- 8D: the eight-disciplines format Arkon uses for the root cause and corrective
  action report.
- PPM: parts per million. The reject rate in PPM is the number of rejected parts
  divided by the number of parts delivered, multiplied by 1,000,000. For example,
  12 rejected parts in 48,000 delivered parts is 250 PPM.
- Lot code or date code: the marking on the parts or the packaging that
  identifies the production lot.
