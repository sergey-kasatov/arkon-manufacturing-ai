# Arkon Manufacturing: Complaint Submission Checklist

Document: Complaint Submission Checklist. Version 2026.1. Effective 2026-09-08.
Status: approved for customer use. Owner: Arkon Customer Quality.

Arkon Manufacturing is a fictional company and this checklist belongs to a
simulated quality process.

## 1. Why the checklist matters

A complaint that arrives with every item on this checklist is investigated from
the day it is received. A complaint that arrives without an item is still
received and logged, and it still receives its Arkon reference, but the
investigation waits until the missing item arrives. Arkon's acknowledgement
commitment runs from the moment the complaint is logged whether or not the
checklist is complete.

Submit the complaint with its attachments through the Arkon customer portal or
by e-mail to customer.quality@arkon-manufacturing.example.

## 2. The twelve items

1. Part number and part description. The Arkon part number as printed on the
   delivery note, and the part description. Why: the part number identifies the
   production process and the drawing the parts were made to.
2. Drawing revision. The revision level of the drawing the parts were ordered
   against. Why: a complaint about a dimension is checked against the revision
   that applied to the delivery.
3. Lot code or date code. The marking on the parts or the packaging. Why: the
   lot code identifies the production lot and the delivered quantity affected.
4. Quantity delivered. The number of parts in the delivery or deliveries
   concerned. Why: the reject rate in parts per million is calculated from it.
5. Quantity affected or suspect. The number of parts found defective and the
   number of parts held as suspect. Why: it sizes the containment at the
   customer and at Arkon.
6. Defect description. What was found, on which feature of the part, and how it
   was found, in the customer's own words. Why: the investigation starts from
   the customer's observation, not from a guess.
7. Photographs. Photographs of the defect and of the part marking. Why: a
   photograph settles what a description leaves open.
8. Measurements or test results. Measured values against the specification,
   with the measuring equipment named, where the complaint is about a
   dimension, a material property or a function. Why: the root cause analysis
   needs the measured deviation.
9. Containment at the customer. What the customer has done with the suspect
   parts: quarantined, sorted, returned or held on the line. Why: the
   containment decision covers the customer's stock as well as Arkon's.
10. Customer contact. The name and role of one customer contact for the
    complaint and the written channel to use for it, e-mail or portal. Why:
    every stage change is sent in writing to this contact.
11. Language. German or English, for the acknowledgement, the reports and the
    closure letter. Why: the 8D report is issued in the language the customer
    contact asks for.
12. Prior reference. The Arkon reference of any earlier notice on the same parts
    or the same defect, in the format ARK-INC-#####. Why: a repeat finding is
    investigated against the earlier corrective action.

## 3. What Arkon asks the customer to do while the complaint is open

- Quarantine the suspect parts and do not rework, scrap or return them before
  the containment decision.
- Keep samples of the defective parts available for Arkon to collect for the
  investigation.
- Quote the Arkon reference (format ARK-INC-#####) in every message about the
  complaint. The reference is assigned when the complaint is logged and is
  stated in the acknowledgement message.
- Report any further finding on the same parts as a new complaint with its own
  submission, and quote the earlier reference in item 12.
